# The colour wheel
The traditional painter's colour wheel starts with three primary colours: red, yellow and blue. Mixing two primaries gives a secondary colour: red and yellow make orange, yellow and blue make green, and blue and red make purple. Colours opposite each other on the wheel, such as blue and orange, are complementary colours and make each other look brighter when placed side by side.

# Warm and cool colours
Reds, oranges and yellows are called warm colours and tend to feel energetic and appear to come forward in a painting. Blues, greens and violets are cool colours that feel calm and seem to recede. Artists use this effect to create a sense of depth.

# Perspective in drawing
Linear perspective makes a flat drawing look three-dimensional. Parallel lines that move away from the viewer, like the edges of a road, are drawn so they meet at a vanishing point on the horizon line. Objects are drawn smaller the further away they are.

# Rhythm and tempo in music
Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds and silences in music. The beat is the steady pulse you tap your foot to, and tempo is how fast that beat goes, often measured in beats per minute. A tempo marked allegro is fast and lively, while adagio is slow.

# Melody and harmony
A melody is a sequence of single notes heard as a tune. Harmony is what you hear when different notes are played at the same time, for example in a chord. A major chord usually sounds bright and happy, while a minor chord often sounds sad or serious.
