# Forces and motion
A force is a push or a pull. Forces can change the speed, direction or shape of an object. Newton's first law says an object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity unless an unbalanced force acts on it. Newton's second law links force, mass and acceleration: force = mass × acceleration, so a 2 kg trolley pushed with 6 N accelerates at 3 m/s².

# Gravity and weight
Gravity is the force of attraction between masses. Mass is the amount of matter in an object and is measured in kilograms; weight is the gravitational force on that mass and is measured in newtons. On Earth an object's weight is about 9.8 N for every kilogram of mass.

# Atoms and molecules
All matter is made of tiny particles called atoms. An element contains only one kind of atom, such as oxygen or carbon. When atoms join together by chemical bonds they form molecules: a water molecule is two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom, written H2O.

# Electric circuits
An electric current only flows around a complete circuit. In a series circuit the components are connected one after another, so the same current passes through each of them and one broken bulb stops the whole circuit. In a parallel circuit each component has its own branch, so the other branches keep working if one fails.
